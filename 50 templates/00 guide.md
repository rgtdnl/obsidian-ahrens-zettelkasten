# 50 Templates: fewer decisions, more thinking

**Purpose:** standard note formats so you never decide structure on the fly. One template per note type you actually use.

The book: simplicity is paramount, and reducing decisions frees mental energy for thinking. The standardization of notes in one format is what lets you shuffle, compare, and recombine them.

## What lives here
- **fleeting.md:** a lightweight capture format with a fate checklist (permanent note, attach, or delete). Use it when a napkin isn't enough; skip it when even opening a template is too much friction.
- **literature.md:** the reference up top (source, author, year), notes in your own words with locators, verbatim quotes where the wording matters. Brief is fine: the context is the text.
- **permanent.md:** one idea per note, full sentences, written as if for print. The filename is the claim.
- **project.md:** next actions, notes being drawn on, assembly area. Discarded or archived when the project ends.

## Rules
- One format per note type, used consistently.
- Change a template only when you feel a real need, not for novelty.
- Templates are for structure, not content. Never let them grow into forms.

## Action
1. Start with literature.md and permanent.md on day one.
2. Add fleeting.md and project.md as you need them.
3. Review templates only when a note fights the format.
